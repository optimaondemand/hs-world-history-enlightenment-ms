📖 MODULE OVERVIEW
Who drew the lines across the map, and by what right?
By the close of the nineteenth century, European powers had partitioned nearly the entire African continent among themselves in barely two decades, while pressing hard against China's ability to control its own trade and territory. Week 16 examines both processes together: the 1885 Berlin Conference, where European diplomats divided African territory according to rules they wrote for themselves, and China's attempt to resist a similar pressure through the words of one of its own officials. Week 17 then turns to a different kind of strain: multinational empires, Russia, Austria-Hungary, and the Ottomans, straining to hold together peoples increasingly drawn to the nationalist loyalties this quarter has already traced in Germany and Italy.
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
