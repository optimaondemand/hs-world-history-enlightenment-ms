6.8
Arthur Young, General View of the Agriculture of Norfolk (1804)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Arthur Young wrote this survey for the Board of Agriculture, touring Norfolk personally to record its farming practices, including its unenclosed common land. These excerpts come from his chapter titled “Wastes.” Minor scanning artifacts in the digitized copy have been silently corrected for readability; no wording has been changed.

As you read, notice that Young writes as an advocate making a case for enclosure, not a neutral observer.

📚 FROM GENERAL VIEW OF THE AGRICULTURE OF NORFOLK, CHAPTER X, WASTES

“Nothing can cause more surprize in the minds of many strangers on their first visiting Norfolk, than to find… a region of warren or sheep-walk, scattered with a scanty cultivation, yet highly improveable. This is a capital disgrace to the county, and has been the result of an absurd prejudice in favour of these old heaths for sheep.”

Arthur Young, General View of the Agriculture of Norfolk (1804), Ch. X

“I crossed 400 acres of thick fern, called Eccles Common… being a good farmer, it is to be hoped so fine a tract of land will not long remain in such a horrid state, exhibiting in its spontaneous produce, its great capabilities of yielding corn and turnips most amply.”

Arthur Young, General View of the Agriculture of Norfolk (1804), Ch. X

“The commons are immense at Attleborough… these are all above 100 acres, and some above 260, with many smaller; I was assured that they amount to between 2 and 3,000 acres.”

Arthur Young, General View of the Agriculture of Norfolk (1804), Ch. X
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
warren: land set aside for breeding rabbits or other small game, historically often left otherwise unfarmed.
sheep-walk: open land used for grazing sheep, typically not plowed or cultivated.
capital disgrace: a severe embarrassment, using “capital” in its older sense of “principal” or “major.”
improveable: capable of being made more productive, an important term in the vocabulary of agricultural reformers like Young.
