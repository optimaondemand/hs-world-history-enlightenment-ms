Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

This translation, by Samuel Moore, was authorized by Engels himself and first published in 1888; it is in the public domain. The first excerpt is the pamphlet's opening line; the second is the opening of Chapter I, its central historical claim.

As you read, notice how quickly the authors move from a dramatic opening image to a specific historical argument they intend to defend with evidence, not just rhetoric.

📚 FROM THE MANIFESTO OF THE COMMUNIST PARTY, 1848

“A spectre is haunting Europe—the spectre of Communism. All the powers of old Europe have entered into a holy alliance to exorcise this spectre; Pope and Czar, Metternich and Guizot, French Radicals and German police-spies.”

Marx and Engels, Manifesto of the Communist Party (1848), Opening

“The history of all hitherto existing societies is the history of class struggles. Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman, in a word, oppressor and oppressed, stood in constant opposition to one another.”

Marx and Engels, Manifesto of the Communist Party (1848), Ch. I

“Our epoch, the epoch of the bourgeoisie, possesses, however, this distinctive feature: it has simplified the class antagonisms. Society as a whole is more and more splitting up into two great hostile camps, into two great classes, directly facing each other: Bourgeoisie and Proletariat.”

Marx and Engels, Manifesto of the Communist Party (1848), Ch. I
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
spectre: a ghost or haunting presence, used here figuratively for a feared but not yet fully understood movement.
exorcise: to drive out an evil spirit, used here figuratively for European powers trying to suppress Communism.
patrician / plebeian: the upper and lower classes of ancient Roman citizens.
antagonisms: conflicts or opposing forces.
