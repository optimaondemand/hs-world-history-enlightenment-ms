8.8
Giuseppe Mazzini, To the Young Men of Italy (1848)
Primary Source · Address Delivered at Milan
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Mazzini delivered this address in Milan on July 25, 1848, commemorating the brothers Attilio and Emilio Bandiera and their fellow patriots, executed in 1844 for attempting an uprising against Neapolitan rule in southern Italy. This English translation comes from a 1906 public-domain anthology of famous orations, itself based on a contemporary translation revised for that collection.

Notice how directly Mazzini connects love of country to love of family and to love of God, treating national duty as a moral and even spiritual obligation rather than a matter of political convenience.

📚 FROM TO THE YOUNG MEN OF ITALY

“Love your family; the partner of your life; those around you, ready to share your joys and sorrows; the dead who were dear to you, and to whom you were dear. Love your country. It is your name, your glory, your sign among the people.”

Giuseppe Mazzini, To the Young Men of Italy (1848)

“Why have we not conquered? Why is it that, while our countrymen are fighting for independence in the North, liberty is perishing in the South? Ah, had we all arisen strong in the sanctity of the idea for which our martyrs died; had the holy standard of their faith inspired our youth to battle; had we made of our every thought an action, and of our every action a thought; had we learned from them that liberty and independence are one, we should not now have war, but victory.”

Giuseppe Mazzini, To the Young Men of Italy (1848)
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
sanctity: the quality of being sacred or holy, treated by Mazzini here as belonging to the cause his martyrs died for.
martyrs: people who suffer or die for a cause or belief, in this case the Bandiera brothers and their fellow executed patriots.
Cosenza: the southern Italian city where the Bandiera brothers and their companions were captured and executed in 1844.
