8.5
Otto von Bismarck, Speech to the Prussian Budget Committee (September 30, 1862)
Primary Source · Known as the "Blood and Iron" Speech
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Bismarck delivered this speech to the budget committee of the Prussian lower house just weeks after becoming Minister-President, during the standoff over army funding described in Lesson 8.3. This English translation is drawn from a 1908 public-domain sourcebook, itself translating from a contemporary German record of the speech; minor spelling artifacts from the original scan have been silently corrected without altering any wording.

Notice that Bismarck spends as much time defending the Prussian constitutional system, as he understood it, as he does making his famous claim about iron and blood. Read for both parts of his argument, not just the memorable phrase.

📚 FROM THE SPEECH TO THE BUDGET COMMITTEE

“This conflict has been viewed too tragically, and taken too seriously by the press; the government seeks no conflict. Could the crisis be avoided with honor, the government would gladly welcome it. But Prussia must keep her strength intact for the favorable moment, which is too often missed. Prussia's boundaries are not favorable to the development of a strong body politic. Not through fine speeches and majority resolutions will the questions of the hour be decided, that was the mistake of 1848 and 1849, but by iron and blood.”

Otto von Bismarck, Speech to the Prussian Budget Committee (September 30, 1862)

“You know that the Prussian ministry in this respect stands in a different position from the English ministry. An English ministry, whatever it may be called, is a parliamentary one, a ministry of a majority of Parliament. We, however, are ministers of his Majesty the king. In denying the separation of the ministry from the crown I am not moved by a desire to make the authority of the crown a shield with which to cover the ministry. We do not need this cloak; we stand fast on the ground of our good right.”

Otto von Bismarck, Speech to the Prussian Budget Committee (September 30, 1862)

“The necessity that the State shall exist is enough for me; necessity alone is authoritative. The Prussian monarchy has not yet fulfilled its mission; it is not yet ready to form a mere ornament for your constitutional structure, to be no more than a useless wheel in the mechanism of parliamentary government.”

Otto von Bismarck, Speech to the Prussian Budget Committee (September 30, 1862)
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
authoritative: carrying the weight of legitimate command, entitled to obedience.
ornament (as Bismarck uses it): something merely decorative, without real power or function.
the mistake of 1848 and 1849: Bismarck's reference to the failed liberal revolutions across the German states, which he believed relied too heavily on speeches and elected assemblies without the force to back their demands.
