6.16
Russia's Industries Report (1893) and Japan's Charter Oath (1868)
Primary Source · Two texts, paired
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

The first excerpt is from The Industries of Russia, an official report commissioned by Russia's Ministry of Finance under Sergei Witte for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, with its introduction written by the chemist D. I. Mendeleev. The second is the Charter Oath issued by the Meiji Emperor in 1868, in the 1914 English translation by W. W. McLaren. Minor scanning artifacts in the digitized Russian report have been silently corrected for readability, with no wording changed.

As you read, notice that one text explains a slow start, and the other announces a fast one.

📚 FROM THE INDUSTRIES OF RUSSIA, INTRODUCTION BY D. I. MENDELEEV (1893)

“The chief cause of the feebleness of the development of the home manufacturing industry consisted for a long time in the whole organisation of former Russian life, which was concentrated in the peasantry, which directed all its energies to agricultural production.”

The Industries of Russia (1893), Introduction

“The rural gentry, or large landholders, having serf labourers bound to them, employed them also mainly in the cultivation of the land and, like the peasants, strove to satisfy their wants as far as possible from their domestic resources, only having recourse to the productions of manufacturing industry as a luxury.”

The Industries of Russia (1893), Introduction
📚 FROM THE CHARTER OATH OF THE MEIJI EMPEROR (1868)

“Deliberative assemblies shall be widely established and all matters decided by open discussion. All classes, high and low, shall be united in vigorously carrying out the administration of affairs of state.”

Charter Oath (1868), Articles I–II, tr. McLaren

“Evil customs of the past shall be broken off and everything based upon the just laws of Nature. Knowledge shall be sought throughout the world so as to strengthen the foundation of imperial rule.”

Charter Oath (1868), Articles IV–V, tr. McLaren
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
feebleness: weakness, here referring to Russia's slow manufacturing growth.
recourse: turning to something for help or use, here referring to relying on manufactured goods only occasionally.
deliberative assemblies: bodies that meet to discuss and decide policy through open debate.
Evil customs of the past: the Charter Oath's own term for outdated practices Japan's new government intended to abandon in favor of Western-style reform.
