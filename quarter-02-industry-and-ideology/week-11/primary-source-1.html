6.13
James Watt (1769) and Henry Bessemer (1905), In Their Own Words
Primary Source · Two texts, paired
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

The first excerpt is Watt's own 1769 patent specification, in his own technical, legal language. The second is from Bessemer's 1905 autobiography, written near the end of his life, recalling the events of August 1856. Minor OCR scanning artifacts in both digitized copies have been silently corrected for readability, with no wording changed.

Notice the difference in voice: one is a formal legal document written to establish a claim, the other a personal memoir written decades later, remembering both triumph and an awkward moment of being underestimated.

📚 FROM WATT'S PATENT SPECIFICATION, 1769

“My method of lessening the consumption of steam, and consequently fuel, in fire engines, consists of the following principles: First, that vessel in which the powers of steam are to be employed to work the engine… which I call the Steam Vessel, must, during the whole time the engine is at work, be kept as hot as the steam that enters it.”

James Watt, Patent Specification, January 5, 1769

“Secondly, in engines that are to be worked wholly or partially by condensation of steam, the steam is to be condensed in vessels distinct from the steam vessels or cylinders, although occasionally communicating with them: these vessels I call Condensers; and, whilst the engines are working, these condensers ought at least to be kept as cold as the air in the neighbourhood of the engines.”

James Watt, Patent Specification, January 5, 1769
📚 FROM BESSEMER'S AUTOBIOGRAPHY (1905), ON THE CHELTENHAM MEETING

“[Mr. Rennie said:] 'This is such an important invention that you ought not to keep the secret another day.'… 'The British Association meets next week at Cheltenham, and I advise you strongly to read a paper on that occasion… so important is this new process to all engineers that, if you will write a paper, I will take upon myself the responsibility of putting it first on the list.'”

Sir Henry Bessemer, F.R.S.: An Autobiography (1905)

“A gentleman who turned out to be Mr. Budd, a well-known Welsh ironmaker, came up to the breakfast-table… 'Do you know, that there is actually a fellow come down from London to read a paper on the manufacture of malleable iron without fuel? Ha, ha, ha!'”

Sir Henry Bessemer, F.R.S.: An Autobiography (1905)
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
fire engines: the period term for steam engines, as used by Watt himself in his patent.
vessel: here, a container or chamber, such as a cylinder or condenser.
malleable iron: a period term broadly covering wrought iron and steel, as distinct from brittle cast iron.
Mechanical Section: the division of the British Association's annual meeting devoted to engineering and mechanical topics.
