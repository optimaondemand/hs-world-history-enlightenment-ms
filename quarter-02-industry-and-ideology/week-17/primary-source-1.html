9.12
Alexander II, Manifesto Emancipating the Serfs (March 3, 1861)
Primary Source · Selected Passages
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

This is an official government decree, issued by imperial authority; it is in the public domain. As you read, notice how much space the manifesto spends reassuring noble landowners compared to how much it spends addressing the peasants it frees.

Pay close attention to the specific conditions attached to the peasants' new freedom, described in the lesson as "freedom with strings attached."

📚 FROM THE MANIFESTO

“We, Alexander II, by the grace of God Tsar and Autocrat of all the Russias… make known to all our faithful subjects: Summoned to the throne of our ancestors by Divine Providence and the sacred law of heredity, we have promised ourselves with heartfelt sincerity to extend our affection and imperial solicitude to all our faithful subjects, whatever their rank or condition, from the soldier who nobly bears arms in the defense of his country to the humble artisan who faithfully carries on his industry.”

Alexander II, Emancipation Manifesto (March 3, 1861)

“The peasants now bound to the soil shall, within the term fixed by the law, be vested with the full rights of freemen. The landed proprietors, while they shall retain all the rights of ownership over all the lands now belonging to them, shall transfer to the peasants, in return for a rent fixed by law, the full enjoyment of their cottages, farm buildings, and gardens… In return for these allotments the peasant families shall be required to pay rent to the landlords, as fixed by the provisions of the law. Under these conditions, which are temporary, the peasants shall be designated as ‘temporarily bound.’”

Alexander II, Emancipation Manifesto (March 3, 1861)

“At the same time the peasants are granted the right of purchasing their cottages and gardens, and, with the consent of the landlords, they may acquire in complete ownership the arable lands and other lands allotted to them as a permanent holding. By the acquisition of a complete title to the land assigned them, the peasants shall be freed from their obligations toward the landlords for land thus purchased, and thus enter definitively into the class of free peasants and landowners.”

Alexander II, Emancipation Manifesto (March 3, 1861)
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
solicitude: careful attention and concern for someone's well-being.
proprietors (as used here): landowners, specifically the Russian nobility who owned the estates serfs worked.
temporarily bound: the manifesto's own term for peasants who had gained legal freedom but still owed rent to their former landlords under the transitional arrangement.
