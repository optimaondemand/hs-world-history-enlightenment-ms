5.5
The Napoleonic Code, on Its Own Territorial Reach (1804)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

These are the Code's own preliminary articles, the same 1910 public-domain English translation used in Week 8, establishing exactly how far and to whom French law applied. By 1804, “the territory” these articles govern already included Belgium and the Rhineland alongside France's older borders, and versions of this same law would soon be introduced, by decree, in the client kingdoms Lesson 5.3 described.

As you read, notice how differently Article 3's phrase “all the inhabitants of the territory” might sound to a French citizen at home versus a newly annexed or allied subject elsewhere in Europe.

📚 FROM THE PRELIMINARY TITLE, OF THE PUBLICATION, EFFECT, AND APPLICATION OF THE LAWS IN GENERAL

“1. Laws are executory throughout the whole French territory, by virtue of the promulgation thereof made by the First Consul… 2. The law ordains for the future only; it has no retrospective operation.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Preliminary Title, Articles 1–2

“3. The laws of police and public security bind all the inhabitants of the territory. Immoveable property, although in the possession of foreigners, is governed by the French law. The laws relating to the condition and privileges of persons govern Frenchmen, although residing in a foreign country.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Preliminary Title, Article 3

“4. The judge who shall refuse to determine under pretext of the silence, obscurity, or insufficiency of the law, shall be liable to be proceeded against as guilty of a refusal of justice.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Preliminary Title, Article 4
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
executory: legally in force and enforceable.
promulgation: the official act of formally putting a law into effect and publishing it.
retrospective: applying to events that occurred before the law existed.
immoveable property: land and buildings, as distinct from personal or movable possessions.
