3.16
Emmanuel-Joseph Sieyès, What Is the Third Estate? (1789)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Sieyès wrote Qu'est-ce que le Tiers État? in French. No public-domain English translation of the full pamphlet could be located for this course, so the passages below have been translated directly from the original French text for this lesson, checked closely against the 1789 original for accuracy. This is a plain, careful translation rather than a historical published English edition.

As you read, notice how Sieyès structures his entire argument around the three questions he poses at the very start.

📚 FROM WHAT IS THE THIRD ESTATE?

“The plan of this work is fairly simple. We have three questions to ask ourselves: First, what is the Third Estate? Everything. Second, what has it been, until now, in the political order? Nothing. Third, what does it ask? To become something.”

Sieyès, What Is the Third Estate? (1789), opening, translated for this lesson

“What does a nation need in order to survive and prosper? Private labor and public functions… Such are the kinds of work that sustain society. Who carries them out? The Third Estate.”

Sieyès, What Is the Third Estate? (1789), Ch. 1, translated for this lesson

“We will see whether these answers are correct. We will then examine the means that have been tried, and those that must be adopted, so that the Third Estate may in fact become something.”

Sieyès, What Is the Third Estate? (1789), opening, translated for this lesson
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
political order: the formal structure of legal and representative power in a state, as distinct from a group's actual economic or social weight.
public functions: Sieyès's term for government offices and official duties, as opposed to ordinary private labor and trade.
sustain: to keep something going, to provide what it needs to continue existing.
