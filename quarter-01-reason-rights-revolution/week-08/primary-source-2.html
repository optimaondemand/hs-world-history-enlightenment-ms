4.16
Napoleon Bonaparte, Proclamation to the French People (November 10, 1799)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Napoleon issued this proclamation the night of 19 Brumaire, hours after the coup succeeded. This English translation is in the public domain.

Remember, as you read, that this is Napoleon's own account of events in which he was the central actor. Read for what it reveals as well as what it may be shaping.

📚 PROCLAMATION TO THE FRENCH PEOPLE, NOVEMBER 10, 1799

“Frenchmen: On my return to France I found division reigning amongst all the authorities. They agreed only on this single point, that the Constitution was half destroyed, and was unable to protect liberty… The Ancients resolved, therefore, upon the removal of the legislative bodies to St. Cloud. They placed at my disposal the force necessary to secure their independence.”

Napoleon Bonaparte, Proclamation to the French People, Nov. 10, 1799

“I presented myself before the Council of the Five Hundred, alone, unarmed, my head uncovered, just as the Ancients had received and applauded me… The stilettos which had menaced the deputies were instantly raised against their deliverer. Twenty assassins rushed upon me, and aimed at my breast. The grenadiers of the legislative body… ran forward, and placed themselves between me and the assassins.”

Napoleon Bonaparte, Proclamation to the French People, Nov. 10, 1799

“At the same moment cries of ‘Outlaw him’ were raised against the defender of the law. It was the horrid cry of assassins against the power destined to repress them… The factions, intimidated, dispersed and fled. The majority, freed from their assaults, returned freely and peaceably into the hall… and drew up the salutary resolution which will become the new and provisional law of the Republic.”

Napoleon Bonaparte, Proclamation to the French People, Nov. 10, 1799

“Frenchmen, you doubtless recognize in this conduct the zeal of a soldier of liberty, of a citizen devoted to the Republic. Conservative, tutelary, and liberal ideas resumed their authority upon the dispersion of the factions.”

Napoleon Bonaparte, Proclamation to the French People, Nov. 10, 1799
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
stilettos: thin daggers, here used by Napoleon to describe weapons carried by his opponents in the chamber.
grenadiers: soldiers of an elite infantry unit, here the troops loyal to Napoleon who intervened at Saint-Cloud.
tutelary: protective, guardian-like.
salutary: producing good or beneficial effects.
