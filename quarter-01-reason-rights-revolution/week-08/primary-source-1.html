4.13
The Napoleonic Code (1804)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

This English translation is drawn from a 1910 published edition of the Code, in the public domain. The articles below are grouped by subject: civil rights, family law, and contracts, matching the three areas Lesson 4.11 discussed.

As you read, notice how differently the Code treats civil equality among men compared to the legal standing it gives married women.

📚 ON CIVIL RIGHTS

“7. The exercise of civil rights is independent of the quality of citizen, which is only acquired and preserved conformably to the constitutional law. 8. Every Frenchman shall enjoy civil rights.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Articles 7–8
📚 ON MARRIAGE

“212. Married persons owe to each other fidelity, succour, assistance. 213. The husband owes protection to his wife, the wife obedience to her husband. 214. The wife is obliged to live with her husband, and to follow him to every place where he may judge it convenient to reside; the husband is obliged to receive her, and to furnish her with every thing necessary for the wants of life, according to his means and station. 215. The wife cannot plead in her own name, without the authority of her husband.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Articles 212–215
📚 ON AGREEMENTS AND CONTRACTS

“1134. Agreements legally formed have the force of law over those who are the makers of them. They cannot be revoked except with their mutual consent, or for causes which the law authorises. They must be executed with good faith.”

Code Napoléon (1804), Article 1134
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
conformably: in accordance with, following the same form or rule as.
succour (SUCK‑er): help or assistance given in time of need.
plead (in this legal sense): to bring or defend a case in court.
revoked: officially cancelled or withdrawn.
