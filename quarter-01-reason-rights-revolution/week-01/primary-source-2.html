1.8
Newton, the General Scholium to the Principia (1713)
Primary Source
📖 BEFORE YOU READ

Newton added the General Scholium to the second edition of the Principia in 1713, after readers pressed him to explain what gravity actually is and why it exists. This excerpt shows his answer in his own words: first, why he refused to guess at gravity's cause, and second, why he believed the order of the universe pointed to something beyond mathematics altogether.

As you read, look for the exact reason Newton gives for refusing to state a cause for gravity, and notice how he uses the word “hypothesis.”

💡 ABOUT THIS TRANSLATION
Newton wrote the Principia in Latin. The text below is Andrew Motte's 1729 English translation, made two years after Newton's death and still the version most historians treat as authoritative. You will notice spellings and word choices that have shifted since then, such as explain'd for explained and phaenomena for phenomena.
📚 FROM THE GENERAL SCHOLIUM

“The six primary Planets are revolv'd about the Sun, in circles concentric with the Sun, and with motions directed towards the same parts and almost in the same plan… it is not to be conceived that mere mechanical causes could give birth to so many regular motions… This most beautiful System of the Sun, Planets, and Comets, could only proceed from the counsel and dominion of an intelligent and powerful being.”

Isaac Newton, General Scholium (1713), trans. Andrew Motte (1729)

“This Being governs all things, not as the soul of the world, but as Lord over all: And on account of his dominion he is wont to be called Lord God… or Universal Ruler.”

Isaac Newton, General Scholium (1713), trans. Andrew Motte (1729)

“Hitherto we have explain'd the phaenomena of the heavens and of our sea, by the power of Gravity, but have not yet assign'd the cause of this power… hitherto I have not been able to discover the cause of those properties of gravity from phaenomena, and I frame no hypotheses. For whatever is not deduc'd from the phaenomena, is to be called an hypothesis; and hypotheses, whether metaphysical or physical, whether of occult qualities or mechanical, have no place in experimental philosophy.”

Isaac Newton, General Scholium (1713), trans. Andrew Motte (1729)

“And to us it is enough, that gravity does really exist, and act according to the laws which we have explained, and abundantly serves to account for all the motions of the celestial bodies, and of our sea.”

Isaac Newton, General Scholium (1713), trans. Andrew Motte (1729)
💡 WORDS YOU MIGHT NOT KNOW
phaenomena: phenomena, the observable effects Newton is trying to explain.
deduc'd: deduced, reasoned out from evidence.
hypothesis: an explanation proposed before it has been tested or proven.
dominion: rulership or authority over something.
counsel: here, deliberate wisdom or planning, not advice.
