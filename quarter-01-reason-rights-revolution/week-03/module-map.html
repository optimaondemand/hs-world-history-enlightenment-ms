📖 MODULE OVERVIEW
If government is something people agree to, what happens when they stop agreeing?
Module 1 asked how anyone should reason once inherited authority stops being automatically trustworthy. This module turns that same question on government itself. Week 3 sets up the basic problem: Hobbes and Locke both imagine a "state of nature" before government exists, and reach almost opposite conclusions about what people should do about it. Week 4 asks what liberty actually requires once a government exists at all: divided power, a shared general will, or plain toleration of disagreement.
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN

📋 WHAT’S AHEAD
1
Week 3: The State of Nature. Hobbes argues fear of violent death justifies near-absolute government; Locke argues natural rights survive into political society and can justify resistance.
2
Week 4: Power, Will, and Toleration. Montesquieu and Rousseau offer two more answers to how liberty survives inside government; Voltaire asks what toleration actually requires.
